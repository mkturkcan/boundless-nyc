# 02 — Old-Law & New-Law Tenement
> Multiple-dwelling walk-ups, 4–7 stories, on 25-ft (and wider) lots. Three legal generations: **pre-law** (before 1879), **old-law / dumbbell** (1879–1901), **new-law** (1901–1929). Found throughout Harlem (E/W 100s–130s, Lenox, 3rd Ave, 116th St), Lower East Side / East Village, and Williamsburg's south side (Broadway, S 2nd–S 5th, Havemeyer, Grand St) and Bushwick.

---

## 1. MASSING & GEOMETRY

### The three legal generations
| Generation | Years | Lot width | Building depth | Stories | Lot coverage | Light/air |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-law** | –1879 | 6.10–7.62 m (20–25 ft) | 15.2 m (50 ft), later to 21.3 m (70 ft) | 4–5 | 50–70% | none required; dark interior rooms |
| **Old-law (dumbbell)** | 1879–1901 | 7.62 m (25 ft) | 24.4–27.4 m (80–90 ft) | 5–6 | 80–90% | **1.52 m (5 ft) wide air shaft**, 6.1–8.5 m (20–28 ft) deep |
| **New-law** | 1901–1929 | 10.7–15.2 m (35–50 ft), often 2–4 merged lots | 21.3–25.9 m (70–85 ft) | 6–7 | **70% interior lot / 90% corner** | outer court min 3.66 m (12 ft) wide; inner court min 7.32 m (24 ft) least dimension |

### Story count (sampling weights, tenement stock)
| Stories | Weight | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.10 | pre-law survivors |
| **5** | **0.47** | dominant — old-law walk-up limit |
| 6 | 0.30 | old-law + new-law |
| 7 | 0.11 | new-law with elevator or raised basement |
| 8+ | 0.02 | |

Derived from the LPC East Village/Lower East Side Historic District inventory (299 buildings: 141 five-story, 60 six-story, 42 four-story, 43 three-story, 5 seven-story).

### Floor-to-floor
| Level | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Ground floor **with storefront** | 3.35 – 4.27 m | 11 – 14 ft (typ 3.66 m / 12 ft) |
| Ground floor **residential** (raised basement + entry) | 2.74 – 3.20 m | 9 – 10.5 ft |
| Raised basement (where present) | 2.13 – 2.59 m | 7 – 8.5 ft; floor 0.6–1.1 m below sidewalk |
| Typical upper floor | **2.74 – 3.05 m** | **9 – 10 ft** (typ 2.90 m / 9.5 ft) |
| Top floor | 2.74 – 2.90 m | 9 – 9.5 ft |

Unlike rowhouses, tenement floor heights are **near-uniform** above the ground floor (variance ≤ 0.15 m). Only the ground floor differs.

### Total heights
| Config | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| 5-story, store below | 15.2 – 17.4 m | 50 – 57 ft |
| 5-story, residential ground | 14.3 – 16.2 m | 47 – 53 ft |
| 6-story | 18.0 – 20.4 m | 59 – 67 ft |
| 7-story new-law | 21.0 – 23.8 m | 69 – 78 ft |

Add parapet 0.61–1.07 m above the roof deck.

### Sidewalk relationship
| Config | Weight | Setback | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Facade on the property line, no areaway** | 0.58 | 0.0 m | Most common; door directly on the sidewalk |
| Shallow areaway | 0.28 | 0.61 – 1.83 m (2 – 6 ft) | Low iron fence, basement window wells |
| Stoop entrance (pre-law / early old-law) | 0.14 | 1.83 – 3.05 m | 5–9 risers, entry at raised ground floor |

Roughly 11% of historic tenements have had their stoops removed and the entrance dropped to grade (34 documented "stoop removed" in the LPC inventory).

### Dumbbell plan (old-law) — the footprint
```
STREET
┌──────────────────────┐  ← full 7.62 m width, ~8.5 m deep
│                      │
│    front apartments  │
├───┐              ┌───┤  ← indent 0.76 m (2.5 ft) each side
│   │              │   │     paired with the neighbor's indent
│   │  stair/core  │   │     = 1.52 m (5 ft) air shaft
│   │              │   │     shaft depth 6.1–8.5 m
├───┘              └───┤
│                      │
│    rear apartments   │  ← full width again, ~7.5 m deep
└──────────────────────┘
REAR YARD (3.0–6.1 m)
```
Apartments per floor: 2–4 (weights: 4 units = 0.62, 3 units = 0.18, 2 units "railroad" = 0.20). Rooms per apartment: 2–3. Stair core at mid-depth, 2.4 × 3.7 m.

**The air shaft is visible from the street only as a 0.76 m deep vertical notch on each flank** — and only where the neighbor is absent or lower. On complete blockfronts the shaft is invisible; model it only for end-of-row/vacant-lot conditions.

### New-law court types
| Type | Width | Depth | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Outer court (open to street/yard) | 3.66 – 6.10 m (12 – 20 ft) | 4.6 – 9.1 m | Creates an H- or U-plan; **visible from the street** |
| Inner court (fully enclosed) | 7.32 – 12.2 m (24 – 40 ft) least dim. | — | Not visible from street |
| Court between wings, rear | 4.6 – 7.6 m | — | |

An outer court on the street facade is the strongest new-law identifier: the facade steps back 4.6–9.1 m for a 3.66–6.10 m width, usually centered, sometimes paired.

## 2. FACADE COMPOSITION

### Bay count vs lot width
| Lot width | Window bays per upper floor | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 6.10 m (20 ft) | 2 – 3 | 0.15 |
| **7.62 m (25 ft)** | **3** (dominant) or 4 | 0.55 / 0.30 within this class |
| 10.7 m (35 ft) | 4 – 5 | |
| 12.2 – 15.2 m (40–50 ft) | 5 – 7 | |

Bay spacing on center (25 ft lot, 3 bays): **2.29 – 2.54 m (7.5 – 8.3 ft)**. Inter-window pier: **1.02 – 1.37 m (40 – 54 in)**. End pier: **0.36 – 0.61 m (14 – 24 in)**.

### Vertical composition
1. **Ground floor**: storefront (weight 0.55 on avenues/commercial streets, 0.20 on side streets) OR rusticated/stone-clad residential base with central entrance
2. **Beltcourse / string course** at 2nd floor level — height 0.15–0.30 m, projection 0.03–0.08 m
3. **Shaft**: 4–5 near-identical floors of planar brick with punched windows
4. **Beltcourses** at intermediate floors (Neo-Grec, Renaissance Revival) — 1 to 4 of them
5. **Top floor special treatment** (weight 0.42): round-arched or segmental-arched heads, arcaded windows, blind tympana, corbelled brick, balconette sills
6. **Cornice**: boldly projecting, bracketed, galvanized iron
7. **Parapet** behind cornice

Style weights (tenement stock): Renaissance Revival 0.24, Italianate 0.22, Queen Anne 0.17, Neo-Grec 0.15, Romanesque Revival 0.07, Neo-Classical 0.05, altered/stripped 0.10.

### Windows
| Parameter | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Opening width | 0.86 – 1.07 m | 34 – 42 in (typ 0.97 m / 38 in) |
| Opening height (typical floor) | 1.52 – 1.98 m | 60 – 78 in (typ 1.75 m / 69 in) |
| Opening height (ground/parlor level) | 1.83 – 2.29 m | 72 – 90 in |
| Opening height (top floor, when arched) | 1.68 – 2.13 m incl. arch | — |
| **Reveal depth** | **0.10 – 0.20 m** | 4 – 8 in (typ 0.13 m) |
| Sill height above finished floor | 0.71 – 0.86 m | 28 – 34 in |
| Head to floor-above soffit | 0.36 – 0.61 m | 14 – 24 in |
| Muntin width | 0.022 – 0.032 m | 7/8 – 1.25 in |

Muntin patterns: **1/1 = 0.78** (dominant; tenements postdate cheap plate glass), 2/2 = 0.14, multi-pane upper sash (Queen Anne) = 0.05, 6/6 = 0.03.

Replacement-window reality: ~70% of surviving tenements have non-historic sash. Modern white vinyl 1/1 with a slightly **smaller** glass area and a **wider frame** (0.05–0.075 m vs 0.032 m). Mix generations within one building: 0.45 probability that a building has 2+ window types visible.

### Lintels & sills
Frequency from the LPC inventory: **stone lintels 46%** (brownstone 15, generic stone 21, limestone 3, terra cotta 3), **metal (pressed/stamped sheet or cast iron) lintels 25%**, brick/segmental 8%, other/covered 21%.

| Element | Projection | Height | Material notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat stone lintel | 0.03 – 0.08 m | 0.20 – 0.36 m | Brownstone or limestone; incised Neo-Grec lines |
| Pedimented stone lintel | 0.08 – 0.15 m | 0.30 – 0.51 m | Common on 3rd story (Pelham/Renaissance Revival signature) |
| Pressed-metal lintel | 0.05 – 0.13 m | 0.20 – 0.41 m | Stamped sheet metal, painted; hollow — reads flatter |
| Cast-iron lintel | 0.03 – 0.08 m | 0.15 – 0.30 m | At storefronts and ground floor |
| Brick segmental arch | 0.00 – 0.03 m | rise = span/6 to span/8 | Voussoir = 1 rowlock or soldier course |
| Round-arch head (top floor) | 0.03 – 0.10 m | rise = span/2 | 1–2 rowlock courses |
| Stone sill | 0.05 – 0.10 m | 0.10 – 0.15 m | Slope 12° |
| Metal sill | 0.05 – 0.10 m | 0.08 – 0.13 m | |
| **Rounded balconette sill** (Queen Anne/RenRev) | 0.15 – 0.30 m | 0.15 – 0.25 m | Scallop or shell shape; on top-floor arched windows |

### Brick
| Color | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Red ("fine red brick") | 0.65 | Old-law standard, Neo-Grec/Queen Anne |
| Buff / yellow | 0.18 | Renaissance Revival (Pelham, Buttenwieser buildings) |
| Orange | 0.11 | Romanesque Revival |
| Orange iron-spot | 0.03 | speckled |
| Beige / brown / gray | 0.03 | |

Bond: **running bond 0.72**, common/American bond (header course every 6th–7th) 0.24, decorative patterning 0.04. Decorative brickwork on Queen Anne examples: **saw-tooth (dogtooth) courses**, corbelled bands, rusticated/projecting brick patterning, recessed spandrel panels 0.03–0.06 m deep.

Brick unit **0.203 × 0.095 × 0.057 m**; course height **0.0667 m**; 3 courses = 0.20 m. Mortar joint 0.0095 m.

## 3. FIRE ESCAPES (the #1 tenement identifier)

Present on the **front facade of ~70%** of surviving tenements (212 of ~299 buildings in the LPC inventory reference a fire escape). All dimensions are from NY Multiple Dwelling Law §53 / 1 RCNY §15-10.

### Balcony (per floor)
| Parameter | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Clear width (projection from wall) | **0.91 m min** | 3 ft (party-wall balcony may be 0.61 m / 2 ft) |
| Typical built projection | 0.91 – 1.22 m | 36 – 48 in |
| Balcony length (along facade) | 1.83 – 3.66 m | 6 – 12 ft — spans 1–2 window bays, typ 2.44 m |
| Guard rail height | 0.76 – 1.07 m | 30 – 42 in (typ 0.91 m) |
| Rail top member | 0.032 – 0.051 m dia | 1.25 – 2 in |
| Vertical picket dia | 0.013 – 0.019 m | 1/2 – 3/4 in |
| Picket spacing on center | 0.10 – 0.15 m | 4 – 6 in |
| Floor: flat steel bars | 0.025 – 0.038 m wide, 0.025 – 0.032 m gaps | 1–1.5 in bars, 1–1.25 in gaps |
| Structural bracket / outrigger | L-angle 0.064 × 0.064 m, at 1.22 m centers | 2.5 in angle |
| Wall anchor plate | 0.10 – 0.15 m square | 4 – 6 in |
| Balcony floor thickness (total) | 0.06 – 0.10 m | 2.5 – 4 in |
| Vertical position | Balcony floor level = window sill level − 0.15 to −0.30 m | so you step down/over the sill |

### Stair runs (between balconies)
| Parameter | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Angle from horizontal | 45 – 60° | typ 50° |
| Clear width | 0.46 – 0.61 m | 18 – 24 in |
| Riser | 0.19 – 0.23 m | 7.5 – 9 in |
| Tread (open bar grating) | 0.15 – 0.23 m | 6 – 9 in |
| Landing platform at each end | **0.508 m min** | 20 in |
| Platform at the drop-ladder end | **0.914 m min** | 36 in |
| Handrail height | 0.76 – 0.91 m | 30 – 36 in |

Stairs **alternate direction** floor to floor (a "Z" or switchback pattern), or all run the same way with a full-length balcony. Weights: alternating 0.62, same-direction 0.38.

### Drop ladder (the bottom element)
| Parameter | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Width | **0.381 m** | **15 in** (code-fixed) |
| Max length | 4.88 m | 16 ft |
| Rung spacing | ≤ 0.305 m | ≤ 12 in |
| Lowest balcony height above grade | ≤ **4.27 m** | ≤ 14 ft (typ 3.0 – 4.0 m) |
| Guide channels | 2 channels, 0.05 × 0.076 m section, full length | |
| Stowed position | Ladder retracted up, top flush with lowest balcony rail | |
| Counterweight | Optional, 0.15 m dia × 0.30 m cylinder on a pulley | |

**Stowed ladder is the default state (weight 0.94).** Model it as a 0.381 m wide × 2.4–3.7 m tall ladder hanging in guides below/behind the lowest balcony, not deployed to the sidewalk.

### Placement rules
- **Centered on the facade** if the building is 3 bays wide (weight 0.60), covering the middle bay
- **Offset to one side** (weight 0.30) on 3–4 bay facades
- **Two fire escapes** on wide new-law facades ≥ 10.7 m (weight 0.10 of all, but 0.45 of new-law)
- Runs from the **2nd floor to the top floor** (never on the ground floor)
- A **roof-access gooseneck ladder** continues past the top balcony to the parapet on ~35%: 0.381 m wide, 1.5–2.4 m tall, curving over the coping
- Balconies stack in perfect vertical alignment
- On **corner buildings**, the fire escape goes on the **side street**, not the avenue
- Prewar apartment buildings put them on the **rear/courtyard**; tenements put them on the **front**

### Material & finish
Steel/wrought iron, painted. Colors: black `#1E1C1A` (0.38), dark red-brown oxide primer `#5A2E24` (0.22), dark green `#22321F` (0.12), gray `#4A4A46` (0.14), rusted/unpainted `#6B4028` (0.14). Roughness 0.55–0.80, metalness 0.20–0.40. Rust coverage 10–45% on unmaintained, concentrated at anchor plates, bar-floor edges, and ladder guides.

## 4. CORNICE

Material weights: **galvanized sheet iron 0.72**, pressed metal 0.10, terra cotta 0.10, wood 0.05, brick corbelled 0.03. Painted; stamped with stock ornament ordered from catalogues — **identical cornice profiles recur on unrelated buildings**, so reuse 4–6 profiles across a whole neighborhood.

## 5. STOREFRONTS (ground floor)

On avenues and commercial streets 55% of tenements have a storefront; on side streets 20%. Historic composition (per LPC): **wood-framed show windows and cast-iron piers set below a projecting cornice.**

| Element | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Storefront opening height | 2.74 – 3.66 m | 9 – 12 ft |
| Cast-iron pier width | 0.15 – 0.30 m | 6 – 12 in |
| Cast-iron column diameter (round) | 0.20 – 0.30 m | 8 – 12 in |
| Cast-iron lintel/beam depth | 0.20 – 0.36 m | 8 – 14 in |
| Bulkhead / kickplate height | 0.30 – 0.61 m | 12 – 24 in (typ 0.46 m) |
| Show window glass height | 1.83 – 2.44 m | 6 – 8 ft |
| Transom height | 0.30 – 0.61 m | 12 – 24 in |
| Storefront cornice projection | 0.20 – 0.46 m | 8 – 18 in |
| Residential entrance width (beside stores) | 1.07 – 1.52 m | 42 – 60 in |
| Stores per 7.62 m facade | 1 (0.62) or 2 (0.38) | |

Modern alterations (measure these — they dominate what you actually see):
- Roll-down security gates: **14%** of all buildings, ~55% of buildings with storefronts. Hood box 0.30–0.46 m section at 3.05–3.66 m height, side guides 0.076–0.102 m wide.
- Security grilles on windows/doors: **42%** of buildings
- Awnings: **28%**
- Through-wall AC units: 8% documented (real-world current rate much higher — use 0.35 per building, 0.12 per window)
- Through-wall heating/AC vent boxes on each story (a distinctive tenement retrofit): 0.66 × 0.42 m, projection 0.05–0.13 m
- Exposed conduit runs on the facade: **30%** of buildings — 0.02–0.05 m dia, vertical + horizontal runs at the ground floor
- Standpipe siamese connection: 0.15 m dia, 0.46–0.76 m above grade, brass/chrome
- Painted facade: **very high** (261 "painted" references) — assume 0.45 probability the brick is painted at least on the ground floor

## 6. ROOF

- Flat, built-up tar/asphalt or modern white TPO, 2% slope to rear
- Front parapet above deck: **0.61 – 1.07 m (24 – 42 in)**; side/rear parapet 0.30–0.61 m
- Coping: stone, terra cotta, or metal, 0.05–0.08 m thick, 0.30–0.46 m wide
- **Water tower on 6-story buildings and up** (see 10-rooftops.md). 5-story tenements: ~15% have one; 6-story: ~65%
- Stair bulkhead: 2.4 × 3.0 m footprint, **2.44 – 3.05 m (8 – 10 ft)** above roof; brick or metal-clad
- Chimney stacks: 2–4 per building on party-wall lines, 0.46 × 0.91 m section, 0.91–2.44 m above roof
- Roof vents / gooseneck vents: 4–12 per roof, 0.10–0.20 m dia, 0.46–0.91 m tall
- Party-wall fire wall projecting above the roof: **0.15 – 0.61 m** above the deck, running the full building depth — visible as a ridge line between adjacent roofs
- Rear/side blind party wall (end-of-row): see 09-corner-buildings.md

## DETAILS CHECKLIST
Ordered by what makes it read as authentic NYC at 30 m.

1. **Front-facade fire escape**, 0.91–1.22 m deep, 2.44 m long, stacked from the 2nd floor to the roof, with a stowed 0.381 m drop ladder at the bottom, 3–4 m above the sidewalk. This is *the* tenement signature — nothing else says NYC faster.
2. **Deeply projecting bracketed cornice**, 0.51 m out, 0.91 m tall, with 9 brackets on a 25-ft facade — throws a hard shadow band across the top floor.
3. **Three windows per floor on a 7.62 m facade**, perfectly aligned in a rigid grid, with **near-uniform floor heights** (unlike a rowhouse).
4. **0.13 m window reveals** on every opening.
5. **5 stories** — the walk-up limit. Not 4, not 8.
6. **Red brick at the correct coursing**: 0.0667 m course height, running bond, mortar visibly lighter than brick.
7. **Projecting stone or pressed-metal lintels and sills** on every window, each carrying a soot streak below.
8. **Soot and rust streaking under fire escape balconies and anchor plates.**
9. **Ground floor differs from the shaft**: taller (3.66 m), stone-faced or storefront, with a horizontal break at the 2nd-floor beltcourse.
10. **Roll-down gate hood box** at 3.05–3.66 m over storefronts, plus side guide rails, even when the gate is open.
11. **Water tower** on 6-story examples (65%).
12. **Retrofit clutter**: exposed conduit, through-wall AC sleeves, standpipe siamese, security grilles, satellite dish.
13. **Top-floor special windows** on 42%: round or segmental arched heads, sometimes with balconette sills.
14. Parapet 0.61–1.07 m above the roof deck with visible coping.
15. Party-wall fire walls ridging 0.15–0.61 m above the roof between buildings.
16. Efflorescence and mismatched brick patches in the lower 2.5 m.

## COMMON MISTAKES

1. **No fire escape, or a fire escape that looks like a modern balcony.** It must be open steel bar grating 0.91–1.22 m deep, thin pickets at 0.13 m centers, alternating diagonal stair runs at ~50°, and a **stowed** 0.381 m drop ladder — not a deployed one reaching the sidewalk.
2. **Fire escape starting at the ground floor.** It starts at the 2nd floor; the lowest balcony is 3.0–4.3 m above grade.
3. **Diminishing floor heights.** That is a *rowhouse* rule. Tenement upper floors are uniform within 0.15 m; only the ground floor is taller.
4. **Cornice too shallow.** Tenement cornices project **more** than rowhouse cornices — 0.51 m typical, up to 0.76 m — with brackets 0.30–0.61 m deep.
5. **Flush windows.** Recess 0.10–0.20 m and model the jamb returns and the projecting sill.
6. **Wrong story count.** 5 stories is 47% of the stock. A 3-story or 10-story "tenement" is a different typology.
7. **Modeling the dumbbell air shaft as visible from the street.** It is a 0.76 m notch at mid-depth, hidden behind the neighbor. Only expose it on end-of-row or vacant-lot conditions.
8. **Uniform, clean brick.** Add per-brick hue/value jitter (±6% / ±10%), 12–35% patched regions, efflorescence in the lower 2.5 m, and painted ground floors on 45%.
9. **Missing the ground-floor break.** The base must read as a distinct horizontal band — taller, different material, beltcourse above.
10. **All windows identical and all in good repair.** 70% have replacement sash; 45% of buildings show two or more sash generations; add 1-in-25 boarded openings.
11. **Every building unique.** Tenements came from catalogues. Reuse identical cornice + lintel profiles across 2–6 adjacent buildings, then vary brick color and retrofits.
12. **No retrofit clutter.** Real tenements are covered in conduit (30%), AC sleeves, standpipes, grilles (42%), gate hoods, and signage. A pristine facade reads as CG.
13. **Fire escape on the avenue face of a corner building.** It goes on the side street.
14. **Fire escape rails/pickets too thick.** Pickets 0.013–0.019 m, rails 0.032–0.051 m. At 0.05 m they look like a jungle gym.
15. **Solid fire escape floors.** They are open bar grating (0.025–0.038 m bars with 0.025–0.032 m gaps) — light passes through and the whole assembly reads as a semi-transparent dark screen.
16. **Missing the shadow the fire escape casts on the facade.** It is a strong, complex, moving pattern and a huge part of the look.
17. **Cornice paint matching the brick exactly.** It is usually a contrasting cream, dark green, or black — a distinct horizontal band.
18. **No parapet.** A roof slab ending flush at the facade top is wrong; add 0.61–1.07 m of parapet above the deck behind the cornice.
19. **Water tower on a 5-story building** (only 15%) or **missing it on a 6-story** (65% have one).
20. **New-law tenements on 25 ft lots.** The 1901 law made that economically impossible — new-law needs 10.7 m+ of frontage and gets an outer court.
