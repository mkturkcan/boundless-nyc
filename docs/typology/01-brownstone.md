# 01 — Harlem Brownstone Rowhouse
> Attached single-family rowhouses, 3–5 stories over a basement, c.1860–1910. Central Harlem (110th–155th St), Mount Morris Park HD (W 119th–124th, Lenox–Mt Morris Pk W), St. Nicholas HD / "Strivers' Row" (W 138th–139th, ACP Blvd–Fred Douglass Blvd), Hamilton Heights, Astor Row (W 130th). Also applies to Brooklyn's Bed-Stuy / Clinton Hill / Park Slope.

### Bay count vs lot width

| 4.88 – 5.49 m (16–18 ft) | door + 1 window | 2 windows |
| 5.79 – 6.40 m (19–21 ft) | door + 1–2 windows | 3 windows |

### Horizontal divisions (bottom to top)
1. **Areaway wall / fence** at sidewalk line
2. **Basement/rusticated base** — 2.29–2.59 m tall, rock-faced or rusticated coursed brownstone, often a different texture from above
3. **Water table / belt course** at parlor floor level — projection 0.05–0.10 m, height 0.15–0.30 m
4. **Parlor floor** — tallest windows, entrance, door hood/enframement
5. **Belt courses** between upper floors (Neo-Grec, Renaissance Revival) — height 0.15–0.30 m, projection 0.03–0.08 m
6. **Upper floors** — diminishing window heights
7. **Cornice** — projecting, bracketed
8. **Parapet** (behind cornice) or **mansard roof**

## 3. STOOP

The defining element. Two geometries:

### Straight (perpendicular) stoop — dominant pre-1890
| Parameter | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Riser height | 0.165 – 0.190 m | 6.5 – 7.5 in (typ 7 in = 0.178 m) |
| Tread depth | 0.279 – 0.330 m | 11 – 13 in (typ 12 in = 0.305 m) |
| Step count | 6 – 12 | typ 8 – 10 |
| Total rise | 1.07 – 1.83 m | 3.5 – 6 ft |
| Total run (steps only) | 1.83 – 3.66 m | 6 – 12 ft |
| Top landing (platform) depth | 0.91 – 1.52 m | 3 – 5 ft |
| Stoop clear width | 1.22 – 1.68 m | 4 – 5.5 ft |
| Cheek wall thickness | 0.20 – 0.36 m | 8 – 14 in |
| Cheek wall height above tread nosing | 0.15 – 0.30 m | 6 – 12 in (railing sits on top) |

Total projection into areaway = run + landing = **2.7 – 4.6 m**, which is why areaways are 2.4–4.6 m deep. If areaway < 3.0 m, expect an L-shaped stoop.

### L-shaped stoop — Renaissance Revival / Colonial Revival, 1885+
Turns 90° at an intermediate landing (often two landings). Projection reduced to **1.22 – 2.13 m (4 – 7 ft)**. Run parallel to facade for 1.5–2.4 m. Common on Strivers' Row and in Mount Morris Park HD.

### Low stoop / stepless — Beaux-Arts, Anglo-Italianate, English Neo-Classical (1890+)
**1 – 4 risers only** (0.18 – 0.71 m rise). Entrance is at or just above sidewalk grade; the piano nobile moves up one floor and gets balconies. Weight in Harlem stock: 0.12.

### Stoop railings & newels
| Element | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Handrail height above tread nosing | 0.76 – 0.91 m | 30 – 36 in |
| Newel post section (square/box) | 0.15 – 0.28 m | 6 – 11 in |
| Newel post total height | 1.07 – 1.68 m | 42 – 66 in (incl. finial/urn) |
| Baluster / picket diameter | 0.016 – 0.032 m | 5/8 – 1.25 in |
| Baluster spacing on center | 0.10 – 0.15 m | 4 – 6 in |
| Rail top-member section | 0.032 – 0.051 m | 1.25 – 2 in |

Cast iron (crystalline, cast in molds, heavy ornamental — Italianate/Neo-Grec/Gothic) vs wrought iron (thinner, hammered, scrollwork — Federal/Greek Revival/Queen Anne). Sampling: cast iron 0.70, wrought 0.22, non-historic pipe rail 0.08.

### Under-stoop / basement entrance
Recessed door beneath the top landing, set back **0.61 – 1.22 m** from the areaway face. Door 0.81 × 1.98 m (32 × 78 in). Often has a gate/grille. Present on ~90% of high-stoop houses.

## 5. CORNICE

Dominant material: **galvanized sheet iron** (72% of the LPC East Village/LES HD inventory), wood 12%, terra cotta 10%, brownstone 6%. Pressed/stamped sheet metal painted to imitate stone.

| Parameter | Metric | Imperial |
|---|---|---|
| Total projection from facade | 0.30 – 0.61 m | 12 – 24 in (typ 0.46 m / 18 in) |
| Total height (fascia + crown + bed mold) | 0.61 – 1.22 m | 24 – 48 in (typ 0.76 m / 30 in) |
| Frieze / fascia band height | 0.25 – 0.61 m | 10 – 24 in |
| Crown molding height | 0.15 – 0.30 m | 6 – 12 in |
| Bracket spacing on center | 0.61 – 1.22 m | 24 – 48 in (typ 0.84 m / 33 in) |
| Bracket count on 6.10 m (20 ft) facade | 6 – 9 | typ 7 |
| Bracket width (face) | 0.10 – 0.20 m | 4 – 8 in |
| Bracket depth (projection) | 0.25 – 0.51 m | 10 – 20 in |
| Bracket height | 0.30 – 0.61 m | 12 – 24 in |
| Dentil block size | 0.05 – 0.08 m | 2 – 3 in |
| Dentil spacing on center | 0.10 – 0.15 m | 4 – 6 in |
| Modillion spacing on center | 0.20 – 0.30 m | 8 – 12 in |

Style → cornice mapping:
- Italianate: heavy, boldly projecting, **scroll/console brackets**, recessed panels between brackets
- Neo-Grec: **angular** brackets, incised linear ornament, deeply projecting
- Greek Revival: wood, **dentils**, shallow projection (0.20–0.36 m)
- Renaissance/Colonial Revival: simple sheet metal, **modillions + dentils**, moderate projection
- Queen Anne / Romanesque: corbelled brick + terra cotta, sometimes stepped gable/parapet

**The cornice runs continuously across all attached houses in a row at exactly the same height.** Break the line only where a taller/shorter neighbor abuts.

## 6. BAY WINDOWS, ORIELS, PARAPETS, ROOF

### Bay windows (present on 25–40% of Harlem rowhouses)
| Type | Projection | Width | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Three-sided (canted) bay | 0.61 – 1.07 m | 2.13 – 3.05 m | Return angle 45–60°; face = 0.9–1.5 m |
| Bow / round front | 0.46 – 0.91 m | 2.44 – 3.66 m | Radius 1.5 – 2.7 m; Mount Morris Park HD signature |
| Oriel (single-floor, corbelled, not full height) | 0.46 – 0.76 m | 1.52 – 2.44 m | Sits on brackets/corbel; parlor or 2nd floor |
| Angular projecting bay (Neo-Grec) | 0.46 – 0.76 m | 1.83 – 2.74 m | Crisp 45° facets |

Full-height bay (basement→cornice) weight 0.45; parlor-only 0.30; parlor+2nd 0.25.

### Parapet / roof
- **Flat roof**, sloped 2% (1/4 in per ft) to rear. Front edge is the high point.
- Front parapet above roof deck: **0.30 – 0.76 m (12 – 30 in)**, behind the cornice, with metal or stone coping 0.05–0.08 m thick, 0.30–0.46 m wide.
- **Mansard roof** (Second Empire, 1860–1875, weight 0.10 of Harlem stock): slate, pitch **70 – 80°**, height 2.44 – 3.35 m (8 – 11 ft), 2–3 dormers, iron cresting 0.30–0.61 m tall on the deck ridge.
- Chimney stacks: 0.46 × 0.61 m to 0.61 × 1.22 m section, **0.91 – 2.13 m (3 – 7 ft)** above roof, brick, 1–3 per house, on the party wall lines. Clay flue pots 0.20–0.30 m dia, 0.20–0.46 m tall.
- Skylight over stair: 1.2 × 1.8 m, 0.15–0.46 m above roof, hipped glass or flat.
- **No water tower** — rowhouses are under 6 stories, so they use street pressure.

## DETAILS CHECKLIST
Ordered by what makes it read as authentic Harlem at 30 m.

1. **The high stoop and the shadow it casts.** Parlor floor 1.42 m above sidewalk, 9 steps, projecting 2.7–4.6 m into the areaway. Without this it is not a brownstone.
2. **Continuous cornice line across 6–12 attached houses at identical height**, projecting 0.46 m so it casts a hard 0.4 m shadow band on the top floor.
3. **Diminishing floor heights.** Parlor 3.66 m, then 3.20, 2.90, 2.74. Parlor windows must be 30–45% taller than top-floor windows.
4. **Window reveals of 0.14 m.** Every opening must be a recessed box, not a decal.
5. **Cast-iron areaway fence + gate at the sidewalk line**, 1.07 m tall, near-black, pickets 0.02 m at 0.13 m centers — reads as a fine dark screen.
6. **The areaway gap itself**: 3.05 m of open space between sidewalk and facade, floor 0.3 m below sidewalk, with a door under the stoop.
7. **Projecting sills and lintels** at 0.05–0.10 m, each throwing a shadow line and carrying a soot streak below.
8. **Zero gaps between houses** — one continuous wall plane per blockfront with no side windows.
9. **Flat roof with a low parapet + 1–3 brick chimneys**, no water tower, no pitched roof.
10. **Desaturated brown**, `#6B4A3A`. If it looks like chocolate or terracotta, the chroma is too high.
11. **Bay window or oriel on 1 in 3 houses**, projecting 0.46–1.07 m, breaking the wall plane rhythmically not uniformly.
12. **Soot streaks under every sill**, 0.15–0.60 m long.
13. Bracketed cornice: 7 brackets on a 6.10 m facade at 0.84 m centers, each projecting 0.25–0.51 m.
14. Double-leaf wood door, 1.4 m wide, recessed 0.30–0.91 m, with a transom.
15. Iron guard bars on basement windows.

## COMMON MISTAKES

1. **Uniform floor-to-floor heights.** Real rowhouses diminish upward — parlor 3.66 m down to 2.74 m at top, a ratio of 0.75. Uniform 3.0 m floors instantly read as CG.
2. **Flush windows.** Glass coplanar with the facade. Recess the sash **0.10–0.20 m** and model the jamb, head, and sill returns.
3. **Missing or token stoop.** A 3-step stoop against the facade is wrong. The stoop must rise ~1.4 m and project 2.7–4.6 m, or turn 90° as an L-stoop.
4. **Facade on the sidewalk line.** Harlem rowhouses sit **2.4–4.6 m back** with a sunken areaway. Losing that gap kills the whole typology.
5. **Cornice too thin.** A 0.10 m fascia strip is not a cornice. Projection **0.46 m**, height 0.76 m, with brackets at 0.84 m centers.
6. **Perfect repetition.** Identical clones side by side. Keep floor lines and cornice height fixed; randomize ironwork paint, door color, soiling, bay windows, AC units.
7. **Over-saturated brownstone.** `#A05A2C`-type orange is wrong. Weathered brownstone is `#6B4A3A` — low chroma, slightly gray.
8. **Pitched or gabled roofs.** All flat with a parapet. Mansards only on Second Empire (~10%), at 70–80°, not a 35° suburban gable.
9. **Gaps between houses / side windows.** Party walls are shared; no gap, no windows on the flanks (except the end-of-row building, which gets a blind party wall).
10. **Wrong brick scale.** Course height is **0.0667 m** — three courses per 0.20 m. Bricks that read as 0.10 m tall make the building look half-scale.
11. **No areaway fence.** The thin dark iron screen at the sidewalk is a primary silhouette element; 92% of houses have some enclosure.
12. **Modern window frames.** Wide white vinyl frames with a single large pane. Use 1/1 wood sash with a 0.022–0.032 m muntin at the meeting rail and a visible sash frame.
13. **Clean stone.** Zero soot, zero patching, zero spalling. Add soot streaks under every sill and 10–60% patch coverage.
14. **Cornice broken per house.** It runs continuously across the whole row at one height. Only break it where a different-height neighbor abuts.
15. **Symmetrical parlor floor.** The door is off to one side (left or right), not centered — the parlor is door + 1–2 windows, asymmetric.
16. **Forgetting the under-stoop basement door**, present on 90% of high-stoop houses.
17. **Water tower on a 4-story rowhouse.** Tanks only appear above 6 stories.
18. **Uniform bay spacing across the parlor floor.** The door bay is wider than the window bays.
19. **Railings too thick.** Pickets are 0.016–0.032 m. Modeling them at 0.08 m makes the ironwork look like a fence from a game asset pack.
20. **Sharp, unbroken stone edges.** Chamfer/round every exposed arris by 0.005–0.015 m; chip stoop nosings.
