### 3.3 Butt-joint / seam rule (non-negotiable for realism)

- Visible butt seam every **3.66 m** of run. On a 6.10 m facade this means **1–2 seams per course**, never zero.
- Stagger adjacent courses by **≥ 0.61 m (24 in)**; never align seams within **3 courses** of each other vertically.
- Offset sequence: cycle 3 or 4 offsets, e.g. `[0.00, 1.22, 2.44, 0.61]` m, then repeat — real installers work from a small set of cut-offs, so the pattern is quasi-periodic, not white noise.
- Seam appearance: a 0.002–0.004 m step, plus a vertical line 8–15% darker than the field, 0.003–0.006 m wide, running the full 0.102 m course height.
- Corner-to-corner runs shorter than 3.66 m (narrow rear extensions, bay returns) get **no** seam.

## COMMON MISTAKES

1. **Siding exposure too large.** A 0.20 m course reads as a barn. Use **0.102 m (Double 4)** at 0.34 or 0.127 m (Double 5) at 0.26; only aluminum single-course goes to 0.152–0.203 m, at combined weight 0.08.
2. **Perfectly flat siding.** Vinyl hangs loose on nail slots. Apply a **0.003–0.008 m wave at a 1.0–1.5 m wavelength** to every panel, random phase. Non-optional.
3. **No butt seams, or seams stacked in a vertical line.** Seam every 3.66 m; stagger adjacent courses ≥ 0.61 m; never align within 3 courses.
4. **Windows filling the whole original opening.** They are 0.10–0.30 m narrower and 0.15–0.61 m shorter, with flat infill panels making up the difference.
5. **Deep masonry-style window reveals on a sided wall.** The glass sits only **0.05–0.10 m** back from the siding face, not the 0.10–0.20 m of a masonry-bearing wall. On the modern Fedders variant it is 0.02–0.05 m.
6. **A pitched gable or hip roof to the street.** These roofs are **flat at 2% to the rear** behind a parapet. The only front-facing slope is a *false* mansard, 1.22–2.44 m tall, projecting 0.20–0.61 m, and only at probability 0.18.
7. **Missing the AC sleeves.** The through-wall grille at 0.66 × 0.42 m below the sill is the defining ornament of this typology. 2–8 per street facade; on a modern Fedders building, 4–12 in a rigid grid.
8. **Trim matched to the siding.** White trim on colored siding at **0.72**; also offset trim white from the window-frame white by ΔE 3–8 — they are never the same white.
9. **One color for the whole row.** Sample per house. Adjacent identical houses are almost always different colors, with only a 0.16 chance of a shared batch.
10. **A Manhattan high stoop (8–12 risers).** Brooklyn/Queens frame rowhouses have **3–6 risers, total rise 0.50–1.15 m**, and the door lands 0.50–1.15 m above grade.
11. **Wood-plank normal maps at brownstone scale.** The visible relief is a hard **0.010–0.016 m lap step**; the grain embossing is only 0.0003–0.0008 m at a 0.10–0.20 m tile. Do not let the grain dominate the lap.
12. **Saturated siding colors.** NYC vinyl is desaturated — keep HSL saturation below 0.18 for 0.80 of samples. White (0.20) + almond (0.15) + light gray (0.10) alone are half of all houses.
13. **Aluminum siding rendered as bare metal.** It is baked enamel: `metalness 0.00–0.10`, `roughness 0.42–0.60`. Its tell is the **crisper shadow line** (1.3–1.6× vinyl) and dents, not shininess.
