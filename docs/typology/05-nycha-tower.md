# 05 — NYCHA Public Housing Tower (Tower-in-the-Park)

> New York City Housing Authority residential buildings, 4–30 stories, c.1935–1975. Brick-clad reinforced-concrete slabs and cruciform towers set at 12–20% site coverage in demapped superblocks. Harlem/Washington Heights (Polo Grounds Towers, Rangel/Colonial Park, Lincoln, Taft, King Towers, St. Nicholas, Grant, Manhattanville) and Williamsburg (Williamsburg Houses 1938 — NYC Individual Landmark, Cooper Park, Jonathan Williams Plaza, Independence Towers, Bushwick). Punched window grid, no cornice, no fire escapes, no balconies (usually), one central brick penthouse.

Court depth on the 1930s H/T plans: **9.1 – 18.3 m (30 – 60 ft)** deep × 15.2 – 27.4 m (50 – 90 ft) wide, plus a projecting centre section or a **breezeway** (a through-passage at ground level with two concrete columns and a metal door, reached by two flights of steps).

### Building rotation
- **Williamsburg Houses (1938): every building rotated 15° off the street grid** — the zeilenbau move, to sweep air into the courtyards and sunlight into the apartments. Streets are demapped and replaced with 15°-rotated pedestrian "Walks."
- 1948–1975: buildings are **parallel or perpendicular to the surviving grid** in ~70% of developments, **offset from one another** (staggered, not aligned) so no two facades face each other across a short gap. 30% use a 30–45° rotation on part of the site.

Real demappings:
- **St. Nicholas Houses**: W 128th, 129th and 130th Streets demapped between Adam Clayton Powell Jr Blvd and Frederick Douglass Blvd → one 4-block superblock, 127th to 131st, 6.33 ha (15.63 acres).
- **Grant Houses**: W 123rd to W 125th, Broadway to Morningside Ave, 6.09 ha (15.05 acres).
- **Williamsburg Houses**: 12 slum blocks (568 buildings on 349 lots demolished, ~5,400 residents relocated) → **4 superblocks formed by closing two through streets**.
- **Fort Greene Houses**: 21 city blocks → 4 superblocks.

**How the demapping reads on the ground (build these):** the old curb line is gone; sidewalks stop at the site boundary and are replaced by 1.83 – 3.05 m (6 – 10 ft) wide concrete or asphalt paths **inside** the site; the old street bed survives only as a straight asphalt service drive or a linear alignment of trees; some demapped streets carry a pedestrian name ("Ten Eyck Walk," "Stagg Walk"). Surrounding streets keep their normal 18.29 m (60 ft) ROW and continuous rowhouse/tenement street wall — the contrast between that wall and the empty superblock is the whole point.

## 4. FACADE — THE PUNCHED WINDOW GRID

The whole facade is one operation: a flat brick plane with rectangular holes punched in a strict grid, plus a sill and a lintel. There is **no cornice, no belt course, no water table beyond a token concrete band, no ornament of any kind above the ground floor**.

Randomizing the massing or the brick colour between buildings of one development is wrong.

## DETAILS CHECKLIST
Ordered by what makes it read as authentic NYCHA at 30 m.

1. **The empty ground.** 12 – 20% site coverage: 30 – 60 m of grass, asphalt path and mature plane trees between buildings, with **no through streets** and no street wall. Get the coverage wrong and nothing else saves it.
2. **Dead-uniform floor lines from grade to parapet.** Floor-to-floor **2.72 m** everywhere, including the ground floor. No tall lobby storey. The window rows are perfectly, relentlessly evenly spaced.
3. **The punched window grid at 3.20 m on centre**, openings 1.39 m wide × 1.27 m high, recessed **0.11 m**, sill 0.81 m above the floor, spandrel 0.56 m. Nothing else on the wall.
4. **No cornice, no crown, no belt course.** The brick wall meets a 0.30 – 0.61 m parapet with a thin concrete coping and stops. A metal pipe rail sits on top of the parapet at 1.07 m.
5. **One central brick penthouse**, 6.1 – 12.2 m square, 3.05 – 4.57 m tall, dead-centre on the roof, with antennas and dishes on it. Not corner bulkheads, not a wood water tower on legs.
6. **Window AC units on 35 – 70% of openings**, with water stains below. This single element carries most of the "lived-in public housing" read.
7. **Red-brown brick `#8E5647` running bond**, identical on every building in the development, with the 3-course = 0.203 m module and control joints every 9 – 12 m.
8. **The plan-type silhouette.** Cruciform notches, a Y at 120°, or a bare 45 – 85 m slab 13.7 m deep. A pure rectangular box is the least common option, not the default.
9. **Recessed entrance bay with a flat cantilevered slab canopy** projecting 1.22 – 2.44 m at 2.44 – 3.05 m above grade, and a **glass-block column running the full building height above it** on 45% of buildings.
10. **NO fire escapes, anywhere.** All NYCHA buildings are fireproof concrete with enclosed stairs.
11. **NO balconies** — P = 0.08 across the stock, 0.00 before 1953.
12. **Painted steel doors in NYCHA blue or teal** (`#2E5A80`, `#2F6B5A`), with a security screen or four-square-light pattern.
13. **The bent-pipe rail fence**: galvanized 0.038 – 0.048 m pipe, 0.91 – 1.07 m tall, hairpin top returns, posts at 1.83 – 2.44 m, edging every lawn.
14. **Slate sills** (`#4E5257`, 0.038 – 0.051 m thick, projecting 0.019 – 0.038 m) and **soldier-course brick lintels** — the entire ornament budget.

16. **Mature London plane trees 15 – 25 m tall** with 12 – 20 m canopies — after 60+ years the trees are taller than a 6-story building and shade the lower 3 floors.

## COMMON MISTAKES

1. **Building it at pre-war coverage.** NYCHA towers sit at **12 – 20%** site coverage (Polo Grounds 12.68%, St. Nicholas 15.49%, Taft 19.63%), not 60 – 75%. If the building touches the sidewalk or its neighbours, the typology has failed.
2. **A tall ground floor.** Pre-war has a 4.11 m lobby storey; NYCHA does **not**. Floor-to-floor is 2.62 – 2.90 m from grade to roof. The uniform spacing is the identifier.
3. **Floor-to-floor too generous.** Modern residential assumptions of 3.0 – 3.2 m make a 20-story NYCHA tower 8 m too tall. Use **2.72 m** for 1946–1958 and **2.79 m** for 1959–1975.
4. **Adding a cornice or a decorative crown.** There is none. Parapet 0.30 – 0.61 m, concrete coping 0.05 – 0.10 m, metal rail at 1.07 m, done.
5. **Flush windows.** Even the "flat" NYCHA facade recesses its openings **0.10 – 0.15 m** into the brick, with a projecting sill. Coplanar glass is the fastest way to lose it.
6. **Fire escapes.** Never. Fireproof concrete construction, enclosed stairs, always.
7. **Balconies by default.** P = 0.08. Adding balconies to every floor converts NYCHA into a 1970s Mitchell-Lama or a Soviet panel block.
8. **Scattered roof bulkheads and a wood water tower on steel legs.** That is the *pre-war* roof. NYCHA has **one centred brick penthouse**; the tank is usually inside it, and where a tank is exposed it is steel, not wood-stave.
9. **Varying the brick colour between buildings of one development.** All buildings share one brick order. Vary the weathering, not the material.
10. **Making the buildings too shallow or too deep.** The double-loaded corridor locks the wing/slab depth at **12.19 – 16.76 m (40 – 55 ft)**, mode 13.72 m. A 9 m deep slab or a 25 m deep block is structurally impossible for the plan type.
11. **All towers identical in height across a development.** Common but not universal: Grant = 13 **and** 21; Jonathan Williams = 14 **and** 21. Mix heights with P = 0.45.
12. **Aligning buildings in a neat row facing each other.** They are deliberately **offset** so no two long facades face across a short gap — that was the stated advantage of the "in-line" plan.
13. **Keeping the street grid.** The whole point is that 1 – 5 cross streets were **demapped**. No curbs, no crosswalks, no street lights inside the superblock — only interior paths.
14. **Clean brick.** Add 15 – 35% repointing patches, 3 – 15% efflorescence, water stains under every sill, and painted-over graffiti in the ground-floor band.
15. **No air conditioners.** 35 – 70% of openings on the sunny faces carry a 0.56 × 0.35 m window unit projecting 0.25 – 0.40 m.
16. **Ornamented sills and lintels.** Slate or cast-stone sills projecting **0.019 – 0.05 m** and flush soldier-course lintels. Anything with a moulded profile is wrong.
17. **Getting the 1930s wrong.** Williamsburg Houses (1938) is **not** red-brick: it is 4-story, tan/ochre brick, with **exposed concrete spandrel beams at every floor**, 0.305 m blue glazed tile at the entrances, cantilevered stainless canopies, corner windows, and every building **rotated 15° off the grid**. Do not apply the 1955 red-brick tower rules to it.
18. **A brick water table with a moulded profile.** NYCHA's base is at most a plain concrete band 0.20 – 0.46 m tall projecting 0.02 – 0.05 m, top 0.30 – 0.91 m above grade — present on only ~55% of buildings.
19. **Young trees.** These sites are 55 – 90 years old. The plane trees are 15 – 25 m tall with 12 – 20 m canopies and shade the lower three storeys; the lawns have bare worn desire lines across 8 – 20% of their area.
20. **Chain-link everywhere.** The signature enclosure is the **bent-pipe hairpin rail at 0.91 – 1.07 m** around lawns; chain-link is used only at playgrounds, ball courts and service yards.
21. **Symmetric single-entrance towers.** The primary entrance faces the **site interior** on 75% of buildings, with 1 – 3 secondary doors on the perimeter side — so the "front" is not the street side.
22. **Window openings too large.** The dominant NYCHA masonry opening is **1.39 m wide × 1.27 m high** — small and squat. Ribbon glazing or floor-to-ceiling glass belongs to a different typology entirely.
