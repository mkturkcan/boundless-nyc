# 03 — Williamsburg Industrial Loft / Warehouse
> Load-bearing-brick and early-concrete industrial buildings, **4–8 stories**, c.1870–1930, in Williamsburg / Greenpoint / DUMBO / Wallabout: Kent Ave, Wythe Ave, Berry St, N 11th–N 14th St, Broadway (S Williamsburg), Water/Plymouth/Jay/Pearl St (DUMBO), West & Kent St (Greenpoint). Reference set: Havemeyers & Elder / Domino Sugar Refinery (314 Kent Ave, 1882), Austin Nichols & Co. Warehouse (184 Kent Ave, Cass Gilbert, 1914–15), Wythe Hotel (80 Wythe Ave, Theobald Engelhardt, 1901 cooperage), Gretsch Building (60 Broadway, 1916), Smith Gray & Co. Building (103 Broadway, 1870), Eberhard Faber Pencil Factory (Kent/Greenpoint Ave, 1872–1924), Greenpoint Terminal Market / American Manufacturing Co. (1890+), E.W. Bliss (Adams/John St), Robert Gair complex (Washington/Main St), 25 Kent Ave (2019, the modern homage).

### Bay grouping (piers create A/B rhythm — do NOT make every bay identical)
Real Domino grouping patterns, read pier-to-pier: **2/3/2**, **3/2/3/2**, **2/2/1**, **3/3/3**. Rule: divide the facade into 3–5 pier-bounded panels; give the end panels 2 windows and the centre panel 3, or alternate wide (3-window, 4.9–6.1 m) and narrow (1-window, 1.8–2.4 m) bays. Domino Kent Ave: "smooth shallow pilasters rise from the ground story, dividing each floor into **wide and narrow bays**."

## 3. LOAD-BEARING BRICK WALL, TAPER & REVEALS

Wall thickness is a multiple of a **wythe = 0.102 m (4 in)** (0.095 m brick + 0.0095 m collar joint).

### Taper schedule (heavy warehouse series — use this)
| Story (from top) | Thickness | Wythes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (top) | 0.305 m (12 in) | 3 |
| 2 | 0.305 m (12 in) | 3 |
| 3 | 0.406 m (16 in) | 4 |
| 4 | 0.406 m (16 in) | 4 |
| 5 | 0.508 m (20 in) | 5 |
| 6 | 0.508 m (20 in) | 5 |
| 7 | 0.610 m (24 in) | 6 |
| 8 (ground) | **0.711 m (28 in)** | 7 |

Rule of thumb (1899/1916 NYC code lineage): **top story 0.305 m (12 in); add 0.102 m (4 in) for every two stories descending.** Light-loft series starts one wythe thinner; 4-story loft = 0.305 / 0.305 / 0.406 / 0.508 m.

**Extreme reference (do not use for 4–8 story):** Domino Filter/Pan House — *"The walls were four feet thick at the bottom and two feet thick at the top"* = **1.22 m → 0.61 m**.

### Reveal depth (the #1 CG tell — never model flush windows)
| Story zone | Reveal (facade plane → sash face) |
|---|---|
| Top 2 stories | **0.10 – 0.15 m (4 – 6 in)** |
| Middle stories | **0.15 – 0.22 m (6 – 9 in)** |
| Base 2 stories | **0.20 – 0.30 m (8 – 12 in)** |
| Inside a pier-recessed panel (concrete daylight factory) | 0.05 – 0.10 m sash + 0.10–0.20 m panel recess |

The reveal **steps deeper by 0.05 m each time the wall gains a wythe**. The step happens at the floor line and is invisible outside but sets the shadow depth per story — bake it in.

### Bond
| Bond | Where | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Running bond (all stretchers) | primary street facade, post-1890 | 0.60 |
| **Common / American bond** — header course every **6th** or **7th** course | 19th-c. facades, and nearly all side/rear/party walls | 0.36 |
| English / Flemish | rare on industrial | 0.04 |

Course height **0.0667 m (2.625 in)**; **3 courses = 0.20 m (8 in)**. Header course reads as a subtle 0.0667 m horizontal line every **0.40 m (6 courses)** or **0.467 m (7 courses)** — visible at 30 m as a faint striping; include it.

### Recessed / projecting brick patterning (cheap ornament, very Williamsburg)
Domino west wall: *"the bricks are set at different depths, forming rows, rectangles, squares, and chevrons."* Depth offsets **±0.019, ±0.032, ±0.045 m** only (one-quarter, one-third, half a header). Panel sizes 0.40 × 0.40 m to 1.20 × 2.40 m. Chevron/diaper motifs use header units at 45°.

### Segmental arch geometry — exact
Given clear span **S** and rise **r**, radius **R = S²/(8r) + r/2**, included angle **2·asin(S/2R)**:

| r : S | r for S = 1.22 m | **R** | Included angle | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 : 6 | 0.203 m | **0.833 · S** = 1.017 m | **73.7°** | pronounced, German Renaissance Revival |
| **1 : 7** | 0.174 m | **0.946 · S** = 1.155 m | **63.7°** | **the NYC default** |
| 1 : 8 | 0.152 m | **1.063 · S** = 1.297 m | **56.1°** | shallow, late/utilitarian |
| 1 : 10 | 0.122 m | 1.311 · S = 1.599 m | 44.8° | near-jack |
| 1 : 12 | 0.102 m | 1.542 · S = 1.881 m | 37.2° | "flat segmental" |

Rise is **0.12–0.20 m (5–8 in) for a 1.07–1.52 m span**. Springing sits **0 to 0.067 m (0–1 course)** above the top of the jamb.

### Voussoir counts (use these numbers directly)
| Ring type | Radial band depth on facade | Unit face along the arch | Voussoirs, S = 1.22 m, r = S/7 (intrados arc 1.286 m) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Single rowlock ring** (brick end out, 0.095 m radial) | **0.095 m (4 in)** | 0.057 m + 0.0095 joint = **0.067 m pitch** | **19 – 20** |
| Double rowlock ring | 0.190 m (8 in) | as above; outer ring arc 1.392 m | inner 19–20, **outer 21 – 22** |
| Triple rowlock ring | 0.286 m (12 in) | | 20 / 22 / 23 |
| **Soldier ring** ("radiating header brick lintel") | **0.203 m (8 in)** | 0.057 m + joint = 0.067 m | 19 – 20 |
| Soldier laid flat (broad face radial) | 0.203 m | 0.095 m + joint = 0.105 m | **12 – 13** |

Joints are **wedge-shaped**: 0.0032–0.0064 m (1/8–1/4 in) at the intrados, 0.0095–0.019 m (3/8–3/4 in) at the extrados for uncut rectangular brick. Max joint 0.019 m, min 0.0032 m.

**Arch depth minimums (BIA Technical Notes 31):** segmental/semicircular — depth ≥ **0.0254 m (1 in) per 0.305 m (1 ft) of span, minimum 0.102 m (4 in)**. Jack arch — depth ≥ **0.102 m + 0.0254 m per 0.305 m of span, minimum 0.203 m (8 in)**; jack-arch depth equals **3, 4 or 5 courses** of the surrounding brickwork (0.20 / 0.267 / 0.333 m). ⇒ a 1.22 m (4 ft) span gets ONE rowlock ring; a 2.44 m (8 ft) dock opening gets **two** rings (0.203 m).

### Jack (flat) arch
Camber **0.0104 m per 1.0 m of span** (1/8 in per ft) — 0.013 m over a 1.22 m span; visually flat, but never model it dead flat. Skewback angle **10–15° from vertical** at each end. Extrados and springing must land on horizontal mortar joints.

### Projecting lintel on corbels (the DUMBO signature)
*"Segmental-arch windows with raised brick lintels supported on corbels."* Build it as: single rowlock arch, then **1–2 courses projecting 0.032–0.045 m** above it as a hood, carried on **2 corbel blocks** (one per jamb), each 0.095–0.203 m wide, projecting 0.032–0.064 m, 2 courses tall. Hood extends **0.06–0.12 m past each jamb**.

### Sills
| Type | Dimensions | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Bluestone / granite slab | 0.13–0.20 m tall × 0.11–0.16 m deep, projecting **0.03–0.06 m**, slope 10–15° | 0.46 |
| Brick rowlock sill | 0.095 m tall, projecting 0.019–0.032 m, slope 12° | 0.31 |
| Cast-iron corbeled sill | 0.10–0.15 m tall, projecting 0.05–0.08 m, on 2 scroll corbels | 0.15 |
| Concrete | 0.10 m × 0.13 m, projecting 0.03 m | 0.08 |

### Iron shutter hardware (extremely common, almost always overlooked)
Original industrial windows had **iron shutters**. What survives: **shutter pintles / strap hinges** in the jamb (2–3 per jamb, 0.10–0.15 m long, projecting 0.05–0.08 m) and **shutter dogs / tiebacks** on the wall face beside the opening — a 0.08–0.15 m S- or spiral-shaped iron piece, 1 per side, at 0.6–0.9 m above the sill. Domino: *"the hundreds of windows are supplied with doors [shutters] of the same material [iron]."* Include on 40–70% of pre-1900 openings; rust-streaked.

### Which rows operate
| Vent type | Location | Rows | Opening geometry |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Centre horizontal pivot** (the industrial default) | mid-height, centre of the sash | **2 cols × 3 rows** block, or 1 full row band | pivots about a horizontal axis at mid-height, rotates **60–90°**; top edge swings IN, bottom edge OUT |
| Hopper | bottom **1 row** (sometimes 2) | full width | hinged at bottom, top edge swings **inward 30–45°** |
| Projected / awning | top **1–2 rows** | full width | hinged at top, bottom edge swings **outward 20–35°** |
| Fixed | everything else | | |
| Max ventilator size (Steel Window Institute) | **1.52 m wide × 0.81 m high (5'0" × 2'8")** | | a taller vent must be split |

Row assignment rules: on a **6-row** sash, rows 3–4 form the pivot vent. On an **8-row** sash, rows 4–5 pivot **and** row 1 is a hopper. On a **3-row** (3×3) sash the **middle row** is the pivot. Ganged sash: only the **1st and 3rd (or every other)** unit in a bay gets a vent — do not make all of them operable. Vent frames read **0.019–0.025 m thicker** than fixed muntins: draw a heavier rectangle around the vent.

### Arched-head sash
Where the opening has a segmental or round head, the **top row of lights is cut to the arch**: 3–7 trapezoidal/curved lights following the intrados, with radial muntins converging on the arch centre. The arched top row is **always fixed**.

## 6. CORBELLED BRICK CORNICE & PARAPET

The corbelled cornice is the single most identifying element of the type ("scalloped cornices, horizontal banding, corbelled brickwork, molded surrounds" — LPC on the **American Round Arch style**). Model it as a stack of **brick courses at 0.0667 m** with explicit per-course offsets from the wall plane.

### Variant A — 3-course dentil cornice (small 3–4 story loft) — weight 0.22
| Course | Offset | Note |
|---|---|---|
| +1 | 0.032 m | **dentil course**: headers alternating — unit 0.095 m wide, gap 0.095 m, **pitch 0.190 m** |
| +2 | 0.032 m | continuous |
| +3 | 0.045 m | crown |
Total height 0.20 m, max projection **0.045 m**. Then coping.

### Variant B — 5-course corbel table (the workhorse) — weight 0.34
| Course | Offset |
|---|---|
| +1 | 0.032 m |
| +2 | 0.064 m |
| +3 | 0.095 m |
| +4 | 0.070 m (**set back** — shadow reveal 0.025 m) |
| +5 | 0.121 m (crown) |
Total height 0.333 m, max projection **0.121 m**.

### Variant C — 7-course bracketed corbel cornice (5–8 story, richer) — weight 0.29
| Course | Offset | Note |
|---|---|---|
+1 | 0.032 m | **dogtooth**: units rotated 45° in plan, projecting 0.045–0.067 m, pitch 0.095–0.134 m |
+2 | 0.000 m | flush band |
+3 | 0.038 m | continuous |
+4 | 0.076 m | continuous |
+5 | 0.114 m | continuous — corbel table complete |
+6 | 0.089 m | set back 0.025 m |
+7 | 0.146 m | crown, 2 courses if a heavier cap is wanted |
Total height 0.467 m, max projection **0.146 m**. **Corbel brackets** (per *Building Construction*, Fig. 109): blocks **0.203 m (8 in) wide — one brick — 7 courses high, each course projecting slightly over 0.0127 m (1/2 in)**; spaced at **0.61–1.22 m o.c.**, or 2 per bay aligned over the piers. Cheaper version (Fig. 110): corbels **0.102 m (4 in) wide spaced 0.102 m apart, 3 courses each projecting 0.0095 m (3/8 in), total projection 0.057 m (2-1/4 in)**.

### Variant D — corbelled segmental-arch ("scalloped") cornice — weight 0.15
A run of miniature corbelled segmental arches springing between corbel brackets. Arch span **0.40–0.80 m**, rise **0.10–0.20 m** (r:S ≈ 1:4), single rowlock ring 0.095 m, 6–10 arches per bay-group. ## COMMON MISTAKES

1. **Flush windows.** Reveal must be **0.10–0.30 m**, deepening toward the base. This single error accounts for most "CG warehouse" failures.
2. **Uniform wall thickness.** The wall tapers **0.102 m per two stories** from the base up; the reveal depth must taper with it. A single-thickness extrusion gives a wrong, thin silhouette at the parapet.
3. **Flat-topped rectangular openings everywhere.** 48% of openings are **segmental-arched** and another 13% have a **relieving arch above a straight lintel**. A loft with all-rectangular openings reads as 1960s.
4. **Arches drawn as a swept ring with no voussoir joints.** At 30 m the joints are the arch. Use **19–20 units** across a 1.22 m span, wedge joints 0.0032 m at the intrados widening to 0.0095–0.019 m at the extrados.
5. **Segmental arch too tall.** Rise is **span / 6 to span / 8** — 0.15–0.20 m over a 1.22 m opening, not 0.30 m. A half-round head is a *different* (Rundbogenstil) building.
6. **Cornice as a single extruded slab.** It is **3–7 stepped brick courses** with per-course offsets of 0.032–0.045 m and at least one set-back course. Total projection **0.15–0.45 m**, band height 0.20–0.93 m.
7. **Cornice at the roof deck level.** The cornice sits under a **0.90–1.83 m parapet**; the roof deck is 0.90–1.83 m *below* the coping.
8. **Piers projecting too far.** Industrial pilasters project **0.019–0.045 m** and piers **0.045–0.102 m** — one-quarter to one header. 0.30 m pilasters read as a Beaux-Arts bank.
9. **Every bay identical.** Real facades group bays **2/3/2** or **3/2/3/2** between piers, with alternating wide and narrow bays and "tower-like" heavy end piers.
10. **Modern square/horizontal window panes.** Pre-WWII industrial lights are **vertical** (0.35 w × 0.40 h). Square panes and 0.025 m muntins read as a 2015 loft-style replacement — which is a legitimate *conversion* tell, but not an original.
11. **All sash operable, or none.** One **2×3-light pivot vent** per sash, and only **1/3 to 1/2 of the ganged sash in a bay** get a vent. Vent frames are 0.019–0.025 m heavier than the fixed muntins.
12. **Missing tie rods / star bolts.** Roughly 60% of pre-1900 brick industrial buildings show them, at floor lines, one per 2–3 bays, 0.203–0.254 m across.
13. **Ground floor treated as a shopfront.** The original base is **freight**: docks at 1.22 m, 2.44–4.88 m arched openings, bollards, bumpers, steel angle nosing, and a curb cut 3.66–6.10 m wide.
14. **Loading dock at grade.** Platform height is **1.07–1.32 m**, and the door head is 2.44–4.27 m above the *platform*, not the sidewalk.
15. **Clean, evenly-coloured brick.** Needs per-brick jitter (hue ±4°, value ±12%), 0.6–2.0 m patch drift, 2–6% spalls, 3–10% mismatched repointed patches, and soot at 0.62–0.80 value on protected surfaces.
16. **Graffiti sprayed at any height.** Cap it at **3.0 m** from any standable surface. Roll-down gates get 60–90% coverage; the primary facade gets 5–15%.
17. **Ghost signs at full opacity.** Sun-facing walls read at **0.10–0.25 opacity** with 20–40% of the letter strokes broken up; only a recently-exposed party wall goes above 0.55.
18. **Sawtooth roofs on the tall block.** Sawtooth and monitor roofs belong on **1–2 story wings**, and the glazed face faces **north at 60–90°**. A sawtooth on top of a 6-story loft is wrong.
19. **No parapet step-down on secondary elevations.** Street parapet 0.90–1.83 m; side and rear step down to 0.30–0.90 m in 1–3 steps.
20. **Ignoring conversion.** A modern Williamsburg loft usually has aluminum sash at a 0.02–0.06 m reveal, new punched openings (5–20% of all openings), a dock cut down to grade with a visible concrete seam, and a setback rooftop glass addition. Build the contradiction in.
21. **Painting the whole building one flat colour.** Painted brick is 46% likely — but with 5–25% peel-back to brick, chalking in the washed zones, paint stopping at a hard property-line edge, and paint sagging around every tie-plate boss.
22. **Modeling the side/rear walls with the front's ornament.** Secondary elevations are flat common-bond brick with no pilasters, no belt courses, irregular bay spacing, and often stucco/parging.
